# 39 The completion total

Status: decided 2026-10-31 for the categories, the weights, where it is seen and
the reward. Counts that depend on undesigned systems are marked open.

Part of #196, with `38_achievements.md`. It is the Perfection-style total the
owner asked for on 2026-10-29, and it is the counterpart of Stardew's
perfection tracker.

## What Stardew does

From the saved Perfection Tracker page, read 2026-10-31.

- Eleven categories with fixed weights that add to 100 percent: produce and
  forage shipped 15, obelisks on the farm 4, the gold clock on the farm 10,
  monster slayer goals 10, friends at maximum hearts 11, every skill at level 10
  5, every stardrop 10, every recipe cooked 10, every item crafted 10, every
  fish caught 10, and golden walnuts 5.
- The score is rounded down, so 100 percent shows only when the game is truly
  complete.
- The board sits in a late-game room on Ginger Island, opened with golden
  walnuts. Reaching 100 percent unlocks new content and puts a golden egg in the
  room's shop. The saved page does not say what the new content is.
- Children's friendship does not count toward the friends category, and the
  maximum hearts are 8 for people you can court and 10 for the rest.

## Decisions

Each had a Stardew option.

1. **Stardew's categories minus two, with the hall and gallery added.** The
   obelisks (fast travel) and the gold clock (stops fence decay) have no use
   here, since we have neither fast travel nor fence decay. Their 14 percent goes mostly to hall
   bundles and gallery donations. The owner asked that bundles and collections
   count (`21_fishing.md`, item 13b). The table below gives the exact weights.
2. **On the collections page, always visible.** The total is a percentage with
   each category's progress, next to the achievements. It is rounded down, so
   100 shows only at true completion. This replaces Stardew's late-game board.
3. **The reward is a final hat, a hall scene and a keepsake.** The summit
   achievement's hat (`38_achievements.md`), a short scene at the hall, and a
   keepsake as furniture. No gameplay power. This is ring 1's reward.
4. **Each ring has its own total, plus a valley total.** Ring 1's total is a
   full 100 on its own. Ring 2 (M5) gets its own 100 and its own reward when it
   exists. Ring 3 is unknown (M6, optional, and it may not happen at the scale
   of rings 1 and 2), so it gets a total only if it is built. A player who never leaves
   ring 1 can still finish something. Stardew's single total needs its late
   region for the last 5 percent, which would make ring 2 a requirement for
   100.

## Ring 2 and ring 3

Ring 2 is our counterpart of Stardew's Ginger Island (owner, 2026-10-31).
Ring 3 is unknown, and it may not exist at the scale of rings 1 and 2.

- **Own totals.** Ring 2 gets its own 100, built from what it adds, set out
  in "Ring 2's total" below.
- **Own reward.** Ring 2's 100 gives a haven hat, a haven scene and a keepsake
  (settled 2026-10-31), see below.
- **Stardew's late region.** Ring 2 is the counterpart of Ginger Island, so
  Stardew's golden walnuts are the model for ring 2's collectible. Stardew
  gives them 5 percent of its tracker. Ours gets 25 of ring 2's own 100, since
  the total covers ring 2 alone.
- **Ring 3.** It gets a total only if it is built, with its own categories. The
  valley total counts the rings that exist, so an unbuilt ring costs nothing.
- **Shared categories.** Skills, cooking, crafting and friends are ring 1's.
  A ring that adds recipes or items adds them to its own total, not to ring 1's,
  so finishing ring 1 stays finished.

## Ring 1's finish (settled with the owner, 2026-10-31)

The morning after the total shows 100, the hall steward walks over from the
Outpost and calls the player in (see "How it works"). Stardew's 100 percent adds a stone carving of a stardrop
above Grandpa's Shrine (checked on the saved Stardrop page). Whether it plays a
scene is not checked.

- **The scene is a short one at the hall.** The Long Table is full and the
  villagers wear the hats the player has given them over the years. A toast,
  a few lines, then play resumes. About two minutes. It differs from the
  last-bundle feast (`09_story_and_lore.md`), which is the village thanking
  the player once. This one celebrates the whole valley being tended.
- **Who speaks.** The hall steward gives a short toast that names the mended
  hall. Old Greencoat adds a quiet line that points towards ring 2. Then each
  household head says one line, about 20 in all. The player speaks only
  through a choice of how to take the toast. The scene closes on the steward's
  last line. The line count follows the cast's household count
  (`20_ring1_cast.md`) and is checked against it at writing.
- **Hats.** Every villager wears their own usual hat. No hat is given to
  villagers, so no per-villager gift record exists (owner, 2026-11-10; see
  "Summit build decisions"). Hats are bought and cannot be gifted
  (`38_achievements.md`).
- **The keepsake is a lamp that never gutters,** a plain brass lamp in the
  lamp-makers' style with a small flame that holds steady through ash and wind.
  It is furniture, delivered by mail with the hatter's hat, and works in the
  house. It answers the lamps the Smoulderer's ash dims (`09_story_and_lore.md`).
  No gameplay power.
- **Not chosen:** a model of Hearthwell (art for every building and updates as
  the village changes) and a farm stone carving as Stardew's (it would not be
  furniture).
- **Script text** is written with the cast's voice sheets, not here.

## How it works

- **Where.** The collections page, beside the achievements tab. A total at the
  top, a bar per category, and a tap on a bar lists what is still missing.
- **When it updates.** At day end with the achievement checks
  (`38_achievements.md`).
- **The reward.** At a true 100 percent the summit achievement fires. The next
  morning the hall steward walks over from the Outpost to call the player in for the scene, and the hatter
  sends the hat. The keepsake is placed as furniture by mail.
- **Not counted.** Money, the house, the festivals and the arcade game give
  achievements but are not in the total, as in Stardew.

## Summit build decisions (owner, 2026-11-10)

Each had a Stardew option, and the owner took the recommended one.

- **No hat gift record.** The scene shows each villager in their usual hat.
  Note 61 item 11 (a per-villager gift record) is removed. Giving hats to
  villagers would be its own system, and one two-minute scene does not justify
  it. Stardew has no such record to my knowledge (SC-104, unchecked).
- **Valley summit: designed now, built with ring 2.** The rule above stands
  (mean of the rings that exist, fires when every ring is at 100). Only ring 1's
  summit is built now. With one ring the valley figure would equal ring 1's, so
  the page hides it until ring 2 exists. Stardew has one total only, so it has
  no such figure (checked on the saved Perfection Tracker page).
- **The scene is built with placeholder lines.** The steward's toast, Old
  Greencoat's line and the household lines sit in a data file under
  `src/content/`, like `EndScene`'s placeholder text. Timing is tuned now and
  the real text swaps in with the cast voice sheets. Stardew's 100 percent
  scene is unchecked (SC-104).
- **The lamp arrives by mail.** A mail attachment gains an optional `furniture`
  id (`src/core/mail.ts`), and the lamp is added to the house stock. One core
  change with a test. Stardew mailing furniture and keepsakes is from memory
  (SC-105).
- **Build order for the summit slice.** (1) A `summit` achievement and its hat
  at the hatter, fired by ring 1 reaching 100 (rounded down). (2) The mail
  `furniture` attachment, the lamp furniture entry and the hatter's letter.
  (3) The morning-after trigger: the steward walks from the Outpost and calls
  the player to the hall. (4) The scene with placeholder lines and the player's
  one choice of how to take the toast. (5) Play resumes. Slices 1 and 2 are
  testable without art or the scene. **Slices 1 and 2 are built (2026-11-10):**
  the `summit` achievement (kind `summit`, fires when ring 1 shows 100) with
  the Summit Crown hat at the hatter, the mail `furniture` attachment, the
  `steadfast-lamp` keepsake (flagged `keepsake`, so never sold) and
  `summit-letter`. The achievement cannot fire until the locked counters
  (gallery, friends, elderfruit) are built. **Slice 3 is built (2026-11-10):** `src/core/summit.ts`
  (`SummitCall`, saved with the game) is armed by the night's check when the
  achievement is earned. The next morning the steward calls: the morning report
  shows a placeholder call line (`src/content/summit.json`) and the scene is
  owed. A festival morning is never interrupted: the call waits for the first
  free morning. The walk is told in the report line, not drawn, until art
  exists. The hook for step 4 is `state.summit.scenePending`, read by
  `ReportScene`, which launches the stub `src/scenes/SummitScene.ts`; the real
  scene replaces its body and ends with `summit.finish()`. **Slices 4 and 5 are built (2026-11-10):** `SummitScene`
  plays the Long Table scene from placeholder lines in `src/content/summit.json`
  (17 household lines, the cast's heads bar the steward and Old Greencoat),
  with every person in a usual hat drawn as a placeholder figure. The player's
  one choice (three options) gets a steward reply, the scene closes on the
  steward's line, then `summit.finish()`, a save and play resumes. Script rules
  are in `src/core/summitscene.ts`. The real lines and art wait for the voice
  sheets; the browser look is unchecked.
